Meeting 2: Organizational Friction

Overview

This meeting focuses on the downstream effects of AI. We will discuss the 2025 finding that while AI is improving throughput (speed), it is still negatively impacting stability. We will also look at the "7 Team Profiles" to see where we fit.

Reading Required (Est. 30 min)

  • Source 1 - 2024 DORA Report: Pages 57-68 (Exploring the Downstream Impact of AI)
    • File: 2024-dora-accelerate-state-of-devops-report.pdf
  • Source 2 - 2025 DORA Report: Pages 11-16 (Software Delivery Performance & Team Profiles)
    • File: 2025_state_of_ai_assisted_software_development.pdf

Pre-Work (Optional, 5 min)

Before the meeting, reflect on these questions:

  • How do you personally define "valuable work"?
  • Does AI help you do more valuable work, or just more work?

Facilitator Preparation

Agenda

1. The Shift: Throughput is Improving (10 min)

  • Review:
    • 2024 Finding: AI hurt both throughput and stability.
    • 2025 Finding: AI now improves throughput, but still hurts stability.
  • Discussion: Are we shipping faster? Are we breaking things more often?

2. The Stability Problem (15 min)

  • Review: The persistent negative impact on delivery stability.
  • Hypothesis: We are moving faster than our systems can handle. The "Amplifier" effect is making our instability worse.
  • Discussion: What is our biggest source of instability right now?

3. The 7 Team Profiles (20 min)

  • Review (5 min): The new profiles identified in 2025 (refer to Source 2, Figure 2, page 16):
    • Harmonious high-achievers: High performance, low friction, low burnout
    • Pragmatic performers: Balanced across most factors
    • Stable and methodical: Stable but slower throughput
    • High impact, low cadence: Strong product, low deployment frequency
    • Constrained by process: High friction, burnout from inefficient processes
    • Legacy bottleneck: Constant firefighting, unstable systems
    • Foundational challenges: Low across all performance indicators
  • Activity (15 min total):
    1. Individual assessment (3 min): Each person silently identifies 1-2 profiles that best describe the team
    2. Pair discussion (7 min): In pairs, share your choices and reasoning
    3. Full team convergence (5 min): Poll the team and discuss the top 1-2 profiles
  • Discussion: What is holding us back from being "Harmonious high-achievers"?

4. How Developers Define Value (10 min)

  • Review: The Five views of "value":
    • Utilitarian: Value from solving problems and being useful to others
    • Reputational: Value from peer recognition and status in the community
    • Economic: Value from compensation and career advancement
    • Intrinsic: Value from personal growth, mastery, and learning
    • Hedonistic: Value from enjoyment, fun, and pleasure in the work itself
  • Discussion: Does AI help us achieve the value we care about?

5. Wrap-up & Next Steps (5 min)

  • Summarize key takeaways.
  • Assign reading for Meeting 3: Strategy & Success (Source 1: 69-76; Source 2: 49-64).

Key Questions

  • Why is our stability lagging behind our speed?
  • Which "Team Profile" do we aspire to be, and what is the gap?

Facilitator Notes

If the Team Profile activity causes conflict:

  • Frame as "current state, not permanent identity"
  • Ask: "What would it take to move one profile closer to Harmonious high-achievers?"
  • If there's strong disagreement, capture both views: "Some see us as X, others as Y"

If discussion stalls on throughput vs. stability:

  • Ask concrete questions: "How many production incidents this month? Last month?"
  • "Are we shipping more frequently or just coding faster?"

If no one did the reading:

  • Display Figure 2 (Team Profiles) from Source 2 and walk through it
  • Shorten Section 1 to 5 min, extend Section 3 (profiles) to 25 min

If you run over time:

  • Priority order: Section 3 (Profiles) > Section 2 (Stability) > Section 4 (Value) > Section 1
  • Combine Sections 1 and 2 into 15 min total

Managing the "Five Views of Value" discussion:

  • Don't try to get consensus—different people value different things
  • Capture which view is most dominant on the team (useful for Meeting 3)
